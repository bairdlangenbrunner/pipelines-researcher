# US gas pipelines serving data centers, including lines under 15.5 miles (plan, 2026-10-16)

Goal: every US gas pipeline that supplies the data center buildout is a GGIT row, whatever its length, and carries `DataCenter = yes` with a `DataCenter [ref]`. Background and the funder context are in gem-desk `grants/beyond-carbon/2026-10-16-data-center-pipelines-research-plan.md`. That plan left two questions open, and Baird settled both on 2026-10-16: lines under 15.5 miles (25 km) go into GGIT itself (no supplement tab), and the tag is a yes/blank column with its own ref.

## Why the 2026 US discovery missed them

The October 2 discovery seeded only EIA projects of 15.5 mi (25 km) or more and sent shorter ones to monitor (`batches/united-states-gas/staging/discovery-seeds-20261002/below_floor.csv`, 543 projects). The pipelines that explicitly serve data centers are mostly short laterals: Energy Transfer's Abilene lateral (14 mi), the Tiger lateral (12 mi), Kinder Morgan's Creekside lateral (11.2 mi), Transwestern's Green Chile (17 mi). Many are Texas intrastate lines that never reach FERC, so they barely appear in EIA either.

EIA's August 2026 workbook, 137 current projects, as counted 2026-10-16: 8 name data centers in demand served or notes (6 match a GEM row on the crosswalk, Green Chile and Dalton Lateral II do not). 22 more cite power demand and are under the floor; most are compression-only. EIA alone is a few dozen leads. Most new rows will come from the GOGPT side: 149 US data center gas plants in development, 123 of 153 plant locations within 9.3 miles (15 km) of an existing GGIT route.

## Rulings (Baird 2026-10-16)

1. **`DataCenter = yes` when a validated ref names this pipeline and mentions data centers at all as a demand driver or a reason for the pipeline, its expansion or its upgrade** (loosened by Baird later on 2026-10-16; it first needed a named data center). Data centers listed as one of several demand drivers for this project count, and so does a shipper or utility whose demand for this project the source ties to data centers. Blank with a note in `ResearcherNotes` only when the data center talk is company-wide and not about this project, comes only from project opponents, or is about a different row or system. `DataCenterNotes` says how specific the tie is: a named campus or customer (high), or data centers named among the project's demand drivers (medium). Standing rule 4 applies as usual (one validated ref suffices).
2. **A compression-only expansion tied to data centers is a new row with `LengthKnown = 0`** (Diameter blank, `no route`), in the parent-system / `SegmentName` shape. The parent system row is not tagged for it.
3. **No length floor** for a pipeline tied to data centers. Pipe inside the plant fence and local utility distribution mains are still out of scope (that is a pipeline-type rule, not a length rule).
4. **The columns are tracker-wide** on the Gas tab, so they are never renamed later. Only the US is researched now.
5. **The GOGPT plant pass is in scope**, not deferred.

## Rulings (Baird 2026-10-18)

6. **A tie through a power plant counts.** A pipeline (or its expansion) that supplies a gas plant is `DataCenter = yes` when the plant's own filings, its developer, or the utility tie that plant to data center load. A named campus or customer is high; data center load named among the plant's reasons is medium. Company-wide resource-plan talk about data centers, not about this plant, stays blank as before. Tag the row for the expansion or lateral that serves the plant, not a legacy mainline it hangs off.
7. **Big interstate expansions with an indirect tie are included**, under rule 6 or rule 1, so the blank US in-development rows are re-checked with this rule.
8. **Endpoints are not needed for a data center pipeline row.** A sponsor and a named project are enough (Apollo North, Aristotle North, Clarington Connector, the Stratos Ruby lateral, Polaris Forge and similar); the length and route stay blank until a source gives them. This loosens the country-discovery add threshold for data center leads only, passed through `vetRule`. Loosened further by ruling 10.
9. **US only for now.** The monitor list is a standing deliverable Baird keeps in a Google Sheet, rebuilt by `build_monitor_list.py` in the seeds dir.

## Rulings (Baird 2026-10-19)

10. **The only test for a data center pipeline row is a real pipeline project that serves a data center or the energy supply to one.** A source has to describe the pipeline project (an announcement, filing, permit, or credible press report) and tie it to a data center, directly or through the power plant it supplies (ruling 6). Endpoints, the state, length, and route are not needed. Fill the state when it can be inferred, for example from the sponsor's other assets or the plant it serves, and say in `ResearcherNotes` that it is inferred and why. This replaces the "named project in a known state" leg of ruling 8 (Woodway's 22-mile, 30-inch line for an unnamed hyperscale data center was left monitor on 2026-10-19 for that reason alone). A tie guessed from a line's name is still not a tie, and pipe inside the plant fence and local utility distribution mains stay out (ruling 3).

## Rulings (Amalia, relayed by Baird 2026-10-20)

11. **A gas plant's permit record is a first-line source for the pipeline that feeds it.** Environmental assessments and environmental impact statements for the plant (NEPA documents when a federal agency is involved, and the state equivalents), construction and siting permit applications and the certificates that approve them, the EPA record for the plant, each state's own environmental agency and utility commission filings, and air permit applications and permits (Title V, PSD, minor source) all describe the fuel supply: the pipeline or lateral, its sponsor, size, length, interconnect and route, often with a map. The environmental assessment is the richest of these and is read first; air permits name the supplier and the metering station and sometimes the pipe size. These documents are where the pipeline for a data center plant is most likely to be written down, so every plant seed (step 2, seed C) and every monitor lead with a known plant and no known pipeline gets them searched before the lead is left as not found. Amalia's example (DM 2026-10-20): the USDA Rural Utilities Service environmental assessment for CIPCO's Hickory Grove Generating Station in Scott County, Iowa (March 2026, `rd.usda.gov/media/file/download/usda-rd-hickory-grove-generating-station-ea.pdf`) states the whole gas supply in its project description: about 2,100 feet of new 12 to 16 inch lateral and an interconnection station tying into the Northern Border pipeline, gas delivered by TC Energy, about 750 feet on a new easement, a horizontal directional drill under Hickory Creek, owner CIPCO or a contracted pipeline operator still to be decided, construction from Q1 2027 and operation by Q1 2030. None of that is on the plant's own web page or in GOGPT. The plant was never a seed here, because the plant seeds came only from GOGPT records that mention data centers, and this assessment gives MISO resource adequacy as the need, not data center load; so it is the example of the source type, not a data center pipeline finding. The next plant pass should also take plant seeds from the USDA RUS environmental assessment list (`rd.usda.gov/resources/environmental-studies/assessments`), FERC and state siting dockets, and air permit queues, not GOGPT alone.

## Steps

**Step 0, the columns.** Order matters: the sheet first, then everything that reads the header.
- Live sheet (authorized write only): append two columns to `Gas pipelines` after `SciGridNames` (grid 132 to 134, columns EC and ED). Row 2 there is `=COLUMN()` on every column, not a typed number, so EC2 and ED2 get the same formula (written USER_ENTERED, the one non-RAW write). Row 3 gets the headers `DataCenter` and `DataCenter [ref]` (RAW). Row 1 (the banner) stays blank. Two rows on `Data dictionary - Gas pipelines` (rows 134 and 135): column A is `=INDEX('Gas pipelines'!$A$2:$EB$2, MATCH(...,'Gas pipelines'!$A$3:$EB$3,0))` on every row, so the new rows use the same formula widened to `$ED`, column B is the name, column D the definition. Nine personal filter views end at column 132 and do not widen by themselves; only `PipelineDirectionality` has a dropdown.
- After the write: `gem-db-ops/gem_colmap.py` (`data_center`, `data_center_ref`; adding them earlier would read as a missing column), refresh snapshots.
- Repo, can go first: `DataCenter` takes `yes` or blank (`controlled_vocab.md`, `check_shard_coverage.py`, `check_discovery_staged.py`, `goit-ggit-data-ops/updates/qc/tracker_qc.py`). `ref_pairs.py` needs no change: an exact name match pairs `DataCenter [ref]` with `DataCenter`. A blank `DataCenter` is never an owed fill (not in `FILL_PAIRS_DEFAULT`), because blank is the normal state.

**Step 1, the floor becomes an argument.** `country-discovery` takes `args.minKm` (default 25; 0 = no floor, any sourced length including 0 for compression-only). `check_discovery_staged.py --min-km 0`.

**Step 2, seeds** (`batches/united-states-gas/staging/discovery-datacenter-seeds-<date>/build_seeds.py`), each seed with a source label:
- A. EIA, releases from 2024 on, unmatched on a fresh crosswalk, any length, demand served or notes naming data centers or power. Reversals and abandonments are dropped.
- B. The named leads from the gem-desk web survey: Abilene lateral, Creekside lateral, Amarillo Expansion (CP26-581), the DT Midstream NEXUS interconnect, Homer City supply, Cheyenne Power Hub, Kosciusko Junction.
- C. GOGPT data center plants (fresh pull through gem-db-ops, `Captive industry type = data center` plus a notes search), one seed per plant with coordinates, owner, MW, status, and the nearest GGIT routes. The plant agent finds how the plant gets its gas: a new lateral (a new row), an existing GGIT row (tag it), on-site or behind-the-meter supply with no new pipe (recorded, no row), or not found. Texas plants are checked against Texas Railroad Commission T-4 permits as well as FERC. Every plant's permit record is searched too (ruling 11): its environmental assessment or impact statement, construction and siting permit, EPA and state environmental agency filings, and air permits, since these describe the gas supply pipeline in the most detail.

Run as five regional slices like the October 2 discovery (`seedIntro`/`seedSearch` per source, `priorStaging` = the 10-02 runs, `vetRule` = rulings 1 to 3 above, `minKm: 0`).

**Step 3, tag existing rows.** An update batch over the 81 US in-development pipelines, operating rows with a 2023 or later start, the EIA rows matched to GEM, and the existing rows step 2 points at. Each tag is a `DataCenter` fill with its ref, through the review app and `push.py`.

**Step 4, deliver.** Discovery workbooks, review app, `append_new_rows.py` for the new rows (per-batch authorization), `push.py` for the tags. New laterals arrive routeless; §8 route creation can follow.
