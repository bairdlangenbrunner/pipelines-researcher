I.     Background and Proposal

       PD Transmission is a Texas limited partnership. Upon commencing the
operations proposed in its application, it will become a natural gas company as defined
by section 2(6) of the NGA,3 engaged in the transportation of natural gas in interstate
commerce, and will be subject to the Commission’s jurisdiction under the NGA.
PD Gathering is a Delaware limited partnership that owns and operates natural gas
gathering facilities.4

        PD Gathering owns and operates the non-jurisdictional Anadarko Gathering
System in Texas and Oklahoma, which consists of over 3,000 miles of 2- to 20-inch
diameter pipeline and connects to approximately 2,750 natural gas production wells and
multiple processing plants. The gathering system includes the 150-mile-long, 16- and
20-inch diameter Header Pipeline, which extends from Washita County, Oklahoma, to
Roberts County, Texas, and interconnects with multiple interstate and intrastate pipelines
along its length.5 The Header Pipeline includes one 6,150 horsepower (hp) booster
compression station in Wheeler County, Texas, and one gathering station, which
facilitates the delivery of wellhead gas directly into the Header Pipeline.6

       PD Transmission operates the currently non-jurisdictional PD Pipeline, an
approximately 275-mile-long, 16-inch-diameter natural gas pipeline located entirely
within Texas, which extends from its southern terminus in Nolan County to an
interconnection with the Header Pipeline in Wheeler County. The PD Pipeline includes
one 2,000 hp compressor station in Childress County, Texas, and is capable of
transporting up to 80,000 Dth/d. As currently configured, the PD Pipeline gathers natural
gas owned by PD Gathering for processing and delivery into the Header Pipeline.
Beginning in 2026, PD Gathering plans to shift the processing of the gas currently
gathered along the PD Pipeline to be processed instead in the Midland Basin, closer to its

       A.       Certificate Policy Statement

        Although the Commission’s Certificate Policy Statement was developed to
provide guidance for evaluating proposals to certificate new construction,18 the
Commission also applies the Certificate Policy Statement in assessing applications for
certificate authority to acquire existing facilities including leasing capacity19 and, as is the
case with the PD Pipeline here, to operate existing non-jurisdictional facilities to provide
jurisdictional services.20 The Certificate Policy Statement establishes criteria for
determining whether there is a need for a proposed project and whether the proposed
project will serve the public interest. It explains that, in deciding whether to authorize the
construction of new facilities, the Commission balances the public benefits against the
potential adverse consequences. The Commission’s goal is to appropriately consider the
enhancement of competitive transportation alternatives, the possibility of overbuilding,
subsidization by existing customers, the applicant’s responsibility for unsubscribed
capacity, the avoidance of unnecessary disruptions of the environment, and the unneeded
exercise of eminent domain in evaluating new construction.

        Under this policy, the threshold requirement for applicants proposing new projects
is that the applicant must be prepared to financially support the project without relying on
subsidization from its existing customers. The next step is to determine whether the
applicant has made efforts to eliminate or minimize any adverse effects the project might
have on the applicant’s existing customers, existing pipelines in the market and their
captive customers, and landowners and communities affected by the new facilities. If
residual adverse effects on these interest groups are identified after efforts have been
made to minimize them, the Commission will evaluate the project by balancing the
evidence of public benefits to be achieved against the residual adverse effects. This is
essentially an economic test. Only when the benefits outweigh the adverse effects on
economic interests will the Commission proceed to complete the environmental analysis,
where other interests are considered.

       The Commission views lease agreements differently from transportation services
under rate contracts. The Commission views a lease of pipeline capacity as an
acquisition of a property interest by the lessee in the capacity of the lessor’s pipeline.35
To enter into a lease agreement, the lessee generally needs to be a natural gas company
under the NGA and to obtain a section 7(c) certificate of authorization to acquire the
capacity. Once capacity is acquired, the lessee in essence owns it, and the capacity is
subject to the lessee’s tariff. The leased capacity is allocated for use by the lessee’s
customers. The lessor, while it may remain the operator of the pipeline system, no longer
has any rights to use the leased capacity.36

        The Commission’s practice has been to approve a lease if it finds that: (1) there
are benefits from using a lease agreement; (2) the lease payments are less than, or equal
to, the lessor’s firm transportation rates for comparable service over the term of the lease;
and (3) the lease agreement does not adversely affect existing customers.37 As the
Commission has stated previously:

                 We will not consider any of the prongs of the test in isolation,
                 but rather will balance them on a case-by-case basis. Given the
                 facts of individual lease cases, we will determine whether a
                 proposal meets all of the three established criteria, and, if it
                 does not, weigh the significance of the lease’s failure to satisfy
                 any criterion against the benefits it would provide with respect
                 to other criteria.38

                1.     Lease Benefits

       The Commission has found that capacity leases in general have several potential
public benefits. Leases can promote efficient use of existing facilities, avoid construction
of duplicative facilities, reduce the risk of overbuilding, reduce costs, and minimize
environmental impacts.39 In addition, leases can result in administrative efficiencies for
shippers by eliminating the need to nominate across several pipelines.40

       We find that the proposed lease will enable PD Transmission to utilize excess
available capacity to provide interstate transportation services without constructing new
pipeline facilities that duplicate PD Gathering’s system. By using the proposed lease, PD
Transmission can meet the demands of the market with reduced construction and
environmental impacts.

                2.     Lease Payments

amount to no more than the rate charged by the lessor for a comparable transportation
service.41 Here, PD Gathering does not offer a transportation service that is directly
comparable to the lease payment. In such instances, the Commission has approved lease
agreements where the lessee’s payments are less than, or equal to, what the lessee’s rates
for comparable service would be if the lessee owned or constructed the capacity itself.42
Here, Applicants state that the cost associated with PD Transmission constructing its own
facilities necessary to replicate the lease arrangement is $220,800,000 and would result in
a daily reservation charge of $1.36 per Dth.43 This is significantly higher than the

       C.       Blanket Certificates

        PD Transmission requests a Part 157, Subpart F blanket certificate. A Part 157
blanket certificate gives an interstate pipeline NGA section 7 authority to automatically,
or after prior notice, perform a restricted number of routine activities related to the
construction, acquisition, abandonment, replacement, and operation of existing pipeline
facilities. Because PD Transmission will become an interstate pipeline, we will issue to it
the requested Part 157, Subpart F blanket certificate.

        PD Transmission also requests a Part 284, Subpart G blanket certificate to provide
open-access transportation services.49 Under a Part 284 blanket certificate, PD
Transmission will not require individual authorizations to provide open-access
transportation services to particular customers. PD Transmission filed a pro forma Part
284 tariff to provide open-access transportation services. Since a Part 284 blanket
certificate is required for PD Transmission to offer these services, we will grant a Part
284 blanket certificate, subject to the conditions imposed herein.

       E.       Limited Jurisdiction Certificate

       PD Gathering requests a limited jurisdiction certificate allowing it to lease
capacity to PD Transmission for use in the transportation of natural gas in interstate
commerce under the NGA during the term of the lease, while at the same time leaving
other non-leased gathering operations exempt from NGA jurisdiction.

        To enable PD Gathering to carry out its responsibilities under the lease, we will
issue it a limited jurisdiction certificate. The Commission closely examines proposals
that would create dual jurisdiction facilities, i.e., facilities that would be subject to both
state and federal jurisdiction, to avoid duplicative and/or potentially inconsistent
regulatory schemes over the same facilities. The Commission recognizes that federal
regulation of PD Gathering will be “limited” in that it will apply only to the use of the
relevant portion of its facilities for the transportation of natural gas in interstate
commerce; however, PD Gathering will be subject to exclusive federal regulation with
regard to the leasing of the capacity supporting the 80,000 Dth/day of firm transportation
service that PD Transmission will provide on PD Gathering’s system and any issues that
may arise thereunder.

        The limited jurisdiction certificate will enable PD Gathering to operate the leased
capacity being used for NGA jurisdictional services, subject to the terms of the lease and
subject to PD Transmission’s tariff. The limited jurisdiction certificate will also require
PD Gathering to operate the leased capacity in a manner that ensures PD Transmission’s
ability to provide services on an open-access, non-discriminatory basis. The Commission
