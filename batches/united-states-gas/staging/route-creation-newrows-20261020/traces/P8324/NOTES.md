# P8324 Tiger Gas Pipeline, Richland Parish Lateral (Franklin Farms Lateral)

The map is Figure 1.B.1, General Project Location Map, page 31 of the public Volume I of the ETC Tiger Pipeline Franklin Farms Project application, FERC docket CP26-549-000, accession 20260701-5406, dated May 2026. The file is maps/P8324/PUBLIC-Volume_I_07_01_26.pdf. I rendered page 31 at 300 dots per inch (p31-031.png). The scale is 1 to 336,000, so one pixel is about 28 meters.

The line was extracted by color. The project boundary is drawn as a thick yellow line. I masked the yellow, thinned it to a one-pixel skeleton with trace_yellow.py, and chained the shortest path from the south end to the northeast end with chain.py. The result is 38 points after simplifying to one pixel, saved in trace.json. I looked at overlay.png to confirm that the trace follows the middle of the yellow line.

The yellow boundary has two short side branches. One runs west from the line near US Highway 80 and is about 1.0 mile. The other runs northwest toward Louisiana Highway 852 and is about 2.2 miles. The first version of this trace left both out as probable work areas. That was wrong. The FERC application lists a 1.75 mile, 20 inch Trio Farm Lateral beside the 13.24 mile main lateral, and the sheet has no separate row for it, so the branches belong to this row. The northwest branch matches the Trio Farm Lateral in length and direction. The west branch is not named in the text I read, and it may serve one of the delivery meters. The trace is now a main run plus both branches, each joined to the main run where the skeleton meets it (trace.json, with the earlier main-only trace kept as trace_main_only.json).

The line starts south of Interstate 20, runs north across Interstate 20 and US Highway 80, runs north to a point near Louisiana Highway 583, and then jogs east toward Louisiana Highway 183. The map has two small dogleg jogs on the way north. They are in the trace.

There are six ground control points. All are corners of the Richland Parish boundary, which the map draws as a dashed black line. The longitude and latitude of each corner come from the US Census Bureau TIGERweb county layer for Richland Parish (GEOID 22083), queried on 2026-10-20 and saved as richland_county.json. The pixel position of each corner was measured on the 300 dpi render. None of the coordinates were read off the map. The gcps.json file lists each one with its source reference. Three corners lie north and northeast of the lateral, two lie southeast, and one lies far to the southwest, so the line lies inside the spread of the points.

I tried a seventh point, the northwest corner of the small box on the north boundary. Its pixel distance to the neighboring corner was about 8 percent shorter than the Census distance, so I did not use it. The map may use a slightly different version of the parish line there.

The fit is a first order affine fit. The root mean square error is 0.147 kilometers, the leave-one-out error is 0.458 kilometers, and the largest single residual is 0.259 kilometers. The limit was 5 kilometers. The report is georef_report.json.

The main run is 21.26 kilometers, or 13.21 miles, against the FERC figure of about 13.24 miles for the main lateral. With both branches the whole trace is 26.42 kilometers, or 16.41 miles. The sheet states 12 miles for the main lateral only, so the sheet figure looks low.

The check script is check_P8324.py. It confirms that the whole line is inside Richland Parish. It also finds where the line crosses Interstate 20 and US Highway 80 using Census TIGERweb road lines that were not used as control points. The line crosses Interstate 20 first and US Highway 80 about 1.5 kilometers farther north, which matches the order and spacing on the map. This is an independent check on the position of the middle of the line.

Doubts. The yellow line is a project boundary drawn at a small scale, so it is the pipeline corridor and not the exact centerline. The line width on the page is about 200 meters. The error of the trace against the true pipe is likely a few hundred meters, not tens of meters. The alignment sheets with the exact route are filed as Critical Energy Infrastructure Information and are not public. The east end of the line is a small hook near Louisiana Highway 183 that I kept as drawn. The Appendix A aerial map on page 650 was not used.
